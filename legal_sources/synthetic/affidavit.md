## Affidavit

An affidavit is a written statement of facts that the person making it signs and
swears or affirms to be true before a person authorised to administer an oath,
such as a commissioner of oaths.

In general common-law practice materials, an affidavit is described as a way of
placing evidence before a court where oral evidence is not required. It usually
identifies the deponent (the person making the statement), sets out the facts in
numbered paragraphs, and ends with the deponent's signature and the jurat — the
certificate recording that the statement was sworn or affirmed.

Features commonly listed in general practice materials include:

- a heading identifying the court and the matter;
- the deponent's full name and description;
- numbered paragraphs, each dealing with a distinct fact;
- a statement that the contents are true and correct to the best of the
  deponent's knowledge and belief;
- the signature of the deponent and of the commissioner of oaths.

This description is generic and jurisdiction-neutral. It is not a statement of
Zimbabwean law, and no Zimbabwean instrument has been consulted.
