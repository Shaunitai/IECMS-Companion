## Default Judgment

A default judgment is a judgment given in favour of a claimant because the other
party failed to take a required step within the time allowed — most commonly
failing to enter an appearance or to file a defence.

General practice materials describe two situations in which the expression is
used:

- where the claim is for a liquidated amount and the defendant has not
  responded, so judgment may be granted administratively; and
- where the claim is unliquidated or the relief is not a fixed sum, so the
  matter must still be referred to the court to determine the amount or the
  relief, even though the defendant's failure to respond is admitted.

Rules normally allow the defaulting party to apply to have a default judgment
set aside, and they normally require that application to explain the failure and
to disclose a defence. The time limits and the exact procedure differ between
courts and instruments.

This description is generic and jurisdiction-neutral. It is not a statement of
Zimbabwean law and no time limit stated here should be relied upon.
