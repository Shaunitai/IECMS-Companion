## Notice of Set Down

A notice of set down is a document by which a party informs the court and the
other parties that a matter has been placed on the court's roll for hearing on a
particular date.

In general practice materials it is described as serving two purposes: it puts
the matter before the court for a specific sitting, and it gives the other
parties notice of when they are expected to appear. It typically identifies the
case, the parties, the date and place of hearing, and the party giving the
notice.

Practice differs between courts. In some courts a matter is set down by the
registrar rather than by a party, and the notice may be called something else. A
general description therefore cannot say what is required in a particular court.

This description is generic and jurisdiction-neutral. It is not a statement of
Zimbabwean law.
