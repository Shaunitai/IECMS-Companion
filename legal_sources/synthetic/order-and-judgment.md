## Order of Court

An order of court is a direction made by a court that requires a party, or a
person, to do something or to refrain from doing something. General practice
materials distinguish an order from a judgment by describing the order as the
operative direction, and the judgment as the reasons and findings on which that
direction is based.

## Judgment

A judgment is the formal decision of a court on the matters in dispute. It
normally records the court's findings, its reasons and the relief it grants.
Where reasons are given orally at the hearing and then reduced to writing, the
written version is the record of the judgment.

## The difference in general terms

Described at a general level:

- the **judgment** states the court's decision and the reasons for it;
- the **order** states what is to happen as a result — for example that a sum be
  paid, that a party do or refrain from doing something, or that a matter be
  dismissed.

In practice the two are often issued together and the distinction between them
is not always maintained in ordinary usage. Different courts use different
terminology and different formal requirements.

This description is generic and jurisdiction-neutral. It is not a statement of
Zimbabwean law.
